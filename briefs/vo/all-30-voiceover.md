## Day 1 · What a market is

1. Someone is always on the other side.

2. When you buy, someone sells to you. Forget that, and you'll think price moves by magic.

3. A market is just a place where buyers and sellers meet. Today that place is mostly a screen.

4. Price moves when one side is more eager. Eager buyers push price up. Eager sellers push it down.

5. You're not trading against a chart. You're trading against other people. Many of them are professionals.

6. Respect the other side. Learn before you risk money. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 2 · Where to practice first

1. Don't learn to trade with real money.

2. You wouldn't learn to drive on a motorway. Every beginner mistake costs money. Unless you make it on sim.

3. Sim is a practice account. The same charts. The same prices. Pretend money.

4. Use it to practice one skill at a time. Reading charts. Placing orders. Following rules.

5. Sim won't feel like real money. Your emotions stay quieter. But it shows whether you can follow your own rules.

6. Every lesson in this series, practice it on sim. I'm on sim too. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 3 · Bid, ask and spread

1. Every trade has two prices. Not one.

2. Miss the gap between them, and you pay a cost on every trade without noticing.

3. The bid is the highest price a buyer is offering right now.

4. The ask is the lowest price a seller will accept right now.

5. The space between them is the spread. Buy, then sell straight away, and you lose that gap. Busy markets usually have smaller spreads.

6. Bid. Ask. Spread. Know your cost before you click. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 4 · How to read a candle

1. Can you read this shape?

2. It's called a candle. It's on almost every chart. If you can't read it, you're guessing.

3. One candle covers one slice of time. Inside it are four prices.

4. The thick part is the body. It shows where price opened and where it closed. Green means it closed higher. Red means it closed lower.

5. The thin lines are wicks. They show the highest and lowest price in that time. Colours vary by platform, so check yours.

6. Open. High. Low. Close. Four prices, one shape. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 5 · Timeframes

1. Same market. Different stories.

2. Pick the wrong timeframe, and a small dip can look like a crash.

3. A timeframe is how much time each candle covers. One minute. One hour. One day. You choose it.

4. Short timeframes show every wiggle. Lots of noise. Lots of false alarms.

5. Long timeframes show the bigger direction. Many traders check the bigger one first. Then they zoom in.

6. Pick your timeframe on purpose. Practice it on sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 6 · Up, down, sideways

1. Which way is the market going?

2. Answer that before anything else. Trade against it, and you're swimming upstream.

3. An uptrend makes higher highs and higher lows. Each push goes further than the last. Each dip stops higher.

4. A downtrend is the mirror. Lower highs. Lower lows.

5. No clear steps either way? That's sideways. Price just chops back and forth. It's where forced trades go to die.

6. Up. Down. Sideways. Name it before you trade it. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 7 · Support and resistance

1. Price has floors and ceilings.

2. Nobody draws them for you. Miss them, and you enter right where price tends to turn.

3. Support is a floor. An area where price fell, stopped, and bounced before.

4. Resistance is a ceiling. An area where price rose, stalled, and dropped before.

5. They're zones, not exact lines. Price can poke through and come back. And they do break. Nothing holds forever.

6. Find the floor. Find the ceiling. Then decide. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 8 · What volume tells you

1. Those bars under your chart? That's volume.

2. They aren't decoration. Ignore them, and you can't tell a real move from a weak one.

3. Volume counts how much was traded in each candle. Tall bar, lots of trading.

4. A big move on high volume means many traders took part.

5. The same move on low volume had fewer traders behind it. Treat it with more doubt.

6. Price shows what happened. Volume shows how many showed up. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 9 · Long vs short

1. You can trade a falling market.

2. It's called going short. Mix up the two directions, and you're working against yourself.

3. Going long means you buy first. You gain if price rises. You lose if it falls.

4. Going short means you sell first. You gain if price falls. You lose if it rises.

5. Either way, both directions can lose. Know which one you're in before you click.

6. Long is up. Short is down. Practice both on sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 10 · Market vs limit orders

1. The wrong click costs you money.

2. Click buy the wrong way, and you pay a price you never agreed to. There are two basic order types. They do opposite jobs.

3. A market order says: fill me now. You get speed. You don't control the price.

4. A limit order says: fill me at this price or better. You control the price. It might never fill.

5. Speed or price. You pick one. Know which you picked before you click.

6. Market for speed. Limit for price. Practice both on sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 11 · What slippage is

1. You clicked one price. Got another.

2. That gap has a name. And it costs you.

3. Slippage is the difference between the price you expected and the price you got.

4. It shows up when price moves fast. Like around big news, or when few traders are active.

5. It's part of trading. Calmer markets and limit orders can reduce it. Nothing removes it. So count it as a cost, every time.

6. Expect the gap. Plan for it. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 12 · What a stop loss is

1. Plan your exit before your entry.

2. No exit plan? Then a small loss can grow into a big one. Fast.

3. A stop loss is an order you set before you enter. It closes your trade at a price you pick.

4. Price hits it? You're out. Small loss. You trade again tomorrow.

5. It's not a promise. In a fast market, price can jump past it. That's slippage. But it beats hoping.

6. Exit first. Entry second. Practice it on sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 13 · Where you take profit

1. Know where you'll take profit.

2. Without a plan, traders hold winners too long and watch them turn into losers.

3. A target is the price where you plan to close a winning trade. Many traders set it with a take-profit order.

4. Set it before you enter, at the same time as your stop loss. Exit for a loss. Exit for a win. Both planned.

5. Price may never reach it. That's fine. A target is a plan, not a promise.

6. Two exits before every entry. Practice it on sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 14 · When markets are busy

1. Markets have rush hours.

2. And dead hours. Trade the dead ones, and you sit in slow, choppy price.

3. Many markets trade across time zones. Asia. Then Europe. Then America. Each region is a session.

4. When a big region opens, more traders show up. Moves tend to get bigger, and spreads often get smaller.

5. When two regions are open together, it's often the busiest time of the day. Busier can mean faster. Not safer.

6. Learn when your market is awake. Check its hours before you trade. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 15 · Position size

1. Not where to enter. How much.

2. That's the biggest decision in a trade. Too big, and one normal loss does real damage to your account.

3. Position size is how much you trade. How many shares or contracts.

4. Bigger size means every move in price counts for more. Up and down.

5. So decide the loss you can accept first. Set your stop loss. Then size the trade to fit it. Not the other way round.

6. Start with the smallest size there is. On sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 16 · Risk vs reward

1. Two questions before every trade.

2. What can I lose? What can I make? Skip them, and you'll risk a lot to make a little.

3. Risk is the distance from your entry to your stop loss.

4. Reward is the distance from your entry to your target.

5. Compare them before you click. A small reward for a big risk is a trade to skip. Decide this on every trade, not just some.

6. Know your risk. Know your reward. Then decide. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 17 · Why win rate lies

1. Win most trades. Still lose money.

2. Because win rate is only half the picture.

3. Win rate is how often you win. Nothing more. It says nothing about how much.

4. The other half is size. How big are the wins? How big are the losses? You need both answers.

5. Small wins and one big loss can wipe out a whole week. That's why the stop loss matters.

6. Stop asking how often. Start asking how much. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 18 · Max daily loss

1. One bad day can end an account.

2. You lose. You get angry. You trade bigger to win it back. That's the spiral.

3. The fix is a max daily loss. One number. The most you're allowed to lose today.

4. Set it before the market opens. Calm. Not in the middle of a losing streak.

5. Hit the number? You're done. Close the platform. No last trade. No exceptions.

6. One bad day. One number. Set yours tonight, and practice it on sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 19 · What drawdown means

1. Every trader meets this word. Drawdown.

2. Ignore it, and a bad stretch quietly becomes a blown account.

3. Drawdown is how far your account has fallen from its highest point.

4. The deeper it gets, the harder the climb back. A big hole needs an even bigger gain to fill.

5. So traders set a limit. Reach it, and they stop and review. Before the hole gets deep.

6. Small drawdowns are normal. Deep ones end accounts. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 20 · What leverage is

1. Small money can control big trades.

2. That's leverage. It magnifies everything. Your wins. And your losses. Equally.

3. Here's how. You put down a deposit called margin. It controls a much bigger position.

4. So a small move in price becomes a big move in your account. Up or down.

5. Many products use it. Leverage magnifies losses. They can even be bigger than your deposit.

6. Learn it on sim, with the smallest size. I'm on sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 21 · Futures and micro futures

1. Futures sound scary. They're agreements.

2. But they're leveraged ones. And leverage magnifies losses, so understand them first.

3. A futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell something at a set price on a set date. Traders usually close early and take the difference.

4. Each contract tracks a market. A stock index. Oil. Gold.

5. Many come in smaller micro sizes. Same market, a fraction of the size. Smaller, but still leveraged.

6. Smallest size. On sim first. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 22 · Ticks and pips

1. Price doesn't glide. It steps.

2. It moves in tiny steps. Each step is worth money. Not knowing how much is trading blind.

3. In futures, the smallest step is called a tick.

4. In forex, the common unit is called a pip.

5. Every market sets its own value per step. Look it up on the exchange or your platform before you trade it. With leverage, steps add up fast.

6. Know what one step costs you. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 23 · Your trading plan

1. Trade without a plan, and you're guessing.

2. Guessing feels fine on good days. On bad days, it costs you money.

3. A trading plan is a written page. What you trade. When you trade. How much you risk.

4. It also says how you exit. Your stop loss. Your target. Your max daily loss.

5. Write it when you're calm. Follow it when you're not. Change it only after the market closes.

6. One page. Written down. Test it on sim. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 24 · 3 questions before every trade

1. Pilots use checklists. Traders need one.

2. Before every flight, every time. Without one, you decide everything in the heat of the moment.

3. One. Why am I entering? If you can't answer in a sentence, stop.

4. Two. Where is my exit if I'm wrong? Set it before you enter.

5. Three. How much am I risking? Decide the size last, not first. It has to fit your exit.

6. Three questions. Every trade. No answers, no trade. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 25 · The trading journal

1. One useful trading tool costs nothing.

2. A notebook. Without it, you repeat the same mistake and call it bad luck.

3. A trading journal is a record of every trade. Why you took it. What happened.

4. Add one line on how you felt. Calm. Rushed. Angry. That's where patterns hide.

5. Read it back every week. Your mistakes will repeat. Now you can see them. And fix one at a time.

6. Write it down. Sim trades count too. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 26 · Revenge trading

1. The trade after a loss is dangerous.

2. It's called revenge trading. You're not trading the market. You're trading your anger.

3. It starts the same way every time. A loss stings. You want it back. Now.

4. So you rush in. Bigger size. No plan. That's how one loss becomes three.

5. The fix is a rule made in advance. After a loss, you stop. Walk away. Come back later.

6. The market will be there tomorrow. Make sure your account is too. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 27 · FOMO

1. Price is flying. You want in.

2. Everyone's talking about it. That feeling is FOMO. Fear of missing out. It's a terrible entry signal.

3. By the time a move feels obvious, a lot of it has already happened.

4. Chasing means entering far from any sensible exit. Your stop sits far below you. Your risk gets bigger.

5. So here's the rule. No plan before the move? No trade during it.

6. There's always another trade. There isn't always another account. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 28 · Overtrading

1. More trades doesn't mean more money.

2. Often it means more mistakes. Every trade has a cost. And every trade is a chance to break your rules.

3. Overtrading is trading because you're bored. Or behind. Or itching to click.

4. The warning sign is simple. You can't say why you took the trade.

5. The fix is a limit. A set number of trades per day. Decided before the open. Hit it, and you're done.

6. Fewer trades. Better trades. Survive first. Follow.

## Day 29 · Spotting trading scams

1. Some trading offers are traps.

2. Fall for one, and you can lose money without ever placing a single trade.

3. Red flag one. Promises of profit with no risk. Every real trade has risk.

4. Red flag two. Just copy our signals. You hand over your decisions. Then your money.

5. Red flag three. We're registered. Check that yourself, on the regulator's own website. Not theirs.

6. If it sounds too good to be true, walk away. Survive first. Follow.
